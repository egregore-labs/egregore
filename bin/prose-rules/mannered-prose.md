Mannered prose substitutes metaphor and flourish for direct statement. Instead of "a parameter worth varying," the mannered writer produces "a dial worth turning." Instead of "this point still matters," they write "this point earns its keep." The phrases exist to display the writer, not to convey the idea, and readers can tell. That is why mannered prose irritates: it makes the reader work harder so the writer can perform. It is also imprecise. A metaphor drags in connotations the writer did not choose and cannot control. The fix is to say what you mean. When a literal phrase is available, use it. The external essay register governs its own metaphor through the egregore-voice skill; this rule covers everything a teammate or an agent reads to act.

## Fails

- a dial worth turning
- this point earns its keep
- the hook is the load-bearing wall of the session
- let the signal breathe for a cycle before we act on it

## Passes

- a parameter worth varying
- this point still matters
- the hook runs before every write
- wait one cycle before changing the surface
